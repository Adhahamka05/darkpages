THE WHISPERING WOODS

I thought the scratching was coming from the wind against the cabin walls. I was wrong. The Blackwood Forest doesn't just grow; it breathes. I went out there to find my dog, but the deeper I went, the more the trees seemed to change positions behind me.

By midnight, the path was gone. In its place stood a circle of trees with bark that felt like cold, damp leather. That's when the whispers started. They weren't words at first—just the sound of a thousand dry leaves clicking together. Then, I heard my own voice calling to me from the darkness. "Help me," it cried. I realized with a jolt that the voice wasn't a spirit; it was the trees imitating me, luring me closer to their roots. I looked down and saw the forest floor was white—not with snow, but with the bleached bones of those who heard their own names before me. I am part of the forest now.
