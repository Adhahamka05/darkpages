CRIES IN THE RAIN

In this town, we don't go out when the rain turns grey. We call it 'The Weeping.' If you listen closely to the raindrops hitting your roof, you’ll realize it's not a random rhythm. It's a code. Thousands of tiny voices screaming for help as they hit the ground and shatter.

Last night, I left my window open just an inch. One drop landed on my hand. It didn't feel like water; it felt like a cold, wet tear. I looked at it under a magnifying glass and saw a tiny, distorted face within the liquid, its mouth wide in a silent plea. Now the rain is getting heavier. It’s beating against my door, trying to get in. I can hear them scratching at the glass with their watery fingers. The weather forecast says it won't stop for forty days. I don't think my walls can hold out that long.
