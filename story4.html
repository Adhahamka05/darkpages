THE RED STAIRCASE

I found the door behind the peeling wallpaper in the basement. It led to a spiral staircase that seemed to descend into the very core of the earth, every step painted a deep, wet crimson. Curiosity is a slow-acting poison.

I descended for what felt like miles, counting the steps until the numbers lost meaning. The air grew thick with the smell of iron and wet earth. At step 3,000, I looked back up. The door was a tiny, distant pinprick of light. That's when I heard it from below. *Squish. Thump. Squish. Thump.* Something heavy and wet was climbing up toward me. It didn't have feet; it sounded like a massive piece of raw, moving meat dragging itself up the metal. I turned and ran, my lungs screaming in the hot, stagnant air. I managed to lock the door, but now, every night, I hear a rhythmic *thump, thump, thump* against the basement wood.
